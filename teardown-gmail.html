The deadline that lands in Updates

Gmail sorts mail by who sent it. During placement season, students need it sorted by what they have to do, and by when.

TL;DR Time-bound action emails (test links, registration cut-offs) often come from bulk or automated senders, so they look like updates rather than personal mail. I propose a small, high-precision "Due soon" strip above the inbox, measured by whether more of these emails get acted on before their deadline.

01Who this is for

Final-year engineering students in Indian colleges during placement and internship season, roughly August to March. In this window one inbox mixes:

  • placement-cell circulars sent to hundreds of students at once ("register for the X drive by Friday 5 pm"),
  • assessment invites from applicant-tracking systems ("your test link expires in 48 hours"),
  • job-platform alerts, college newsletters, and promotions.

Only the first two carry a deadline, and missing one usually can't be undone: a closed registration or an expired test link means a lost opportunity, not a late reply.

02What Gmail already does well

Category tabs keep promotions and social mail out of the main inbox. Nudges bring back mail you may have forgotten to answer. Search is excellent once you know what to look for. These solve sorting and replying. They don't solve "what am I about to miss?"

03The friction

Tabs classify by sender type: bulk, automated and mailing-list mail goes to Updates or Promotions. That's usually right, but the emails this user can't afford to miss are exactly the ones sent in bulk:

  • A placement circular to 500 students looks like a mailing list.
  • A test invite from a recruiting platform looks like an automated notification.

The deadline is in the body, not the sender or subject line, so nothing in the inbox view signals it. Nudges are built for replies and arrive after days, which is too late for a 48-hour test link.

Evidence from my own build. I tested my email-triage tool, InboxIQ, on 64 hand-labelled emails. Its biggest weakness was this same line between "sent by a system" and "needs action": with vague definitions, only 6 of 21 person-written, non-customer emails were sorted correctly. Defining categories by intent instead raised it to 19 of 21, and overall accuracy from 91.7% to 99.0%, using a small model at about 1.3 s per email. This shows intent-based sorting is feasible. It doesn't prove the user problem, which section 06 tests.

04Proposed fix: a "Due soon" strip

A slim strip above the inbox, visible from every tab, that lists emails asking the user to do something by a stated date.

Placement Cell: register for the campus driveCloses tomorrow, 5 pmOpenAdd to CalendarDone
Online assessment link: Product Analyst roleExpires in 31 hOpenAdd to CalendarDone

v1 scope, chosen on purpose:

  • Only explicit deadlines ("by 5 pm Friday", "expires in 48 hours"). No guessing at implied urgency.
  • At most 3 items, and an item leaves once its deadline passes or the user marks it done.
  • "Not a deadline" feedback on every item, to catch mistakes and improve the model.
  • Respects the existing smart-features setting; no new data collection.

Trade-off I'm accepting

Precision over recall. One wrong "urgent" item teaches people to ignore the strip. Missing a few deadlines in v1 is better than a noisy strip nobody trusts.

Not in v1

Auto-replies, task lists, deadlines the user would have to infer, and languages other than English. All worth exploring once precision is proven.

Rollout: internal dogfood first to tune precision, then an A/B test on opted-in users in India during peak season, so a 4-week test covers real deadlines.

06How I'd check the problem is real before building

This is a hypothesis. The cheapest test: ask 8–10 final-year students to export the subject lines and tabs of one week of placement email. Then count how many deadline-bearing emails landed outside Primary, and how many were opened only after the deadline.

Kill criterion: if fewer than about 1 in 10 deadline emails land outside Primary, the problem is smaller than I think, and a lighter fix (deadline-aware Nudges) would beat a new UI surface.
